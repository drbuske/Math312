    Slide [STRUCT].[NUM] Some Simple Examples

    When looking at equations in two unknowns, we see that
    🔗
    • One equation has infinitely many solutions.
      🔗
    • Two equations have exactly one solution.
      🔗
    • Three equations have no solutions.
      🔗
    🔗

    Slide [STRUCT].[NUM] Some Simple Examples

    There are exceptions; however, the more equations we have, the smaller the set of solutions will be.
    🔗
    🔗

    Slide [STRUCT].[NUM] Some Simple Examples

    In three unknowns, the set of solutions to one linear equation forms a plane. The set of solutions to a pair of linear equations is seen graphically as the intersection of the two planes.
    🔗
    🔗

    Slide [STRUCT].[NUM] Some Simple Examples

    When we add a third equation, we are looking for the intersection of three planes, which we expect to form a point. It may happen that there are no solutions.
    🔗
